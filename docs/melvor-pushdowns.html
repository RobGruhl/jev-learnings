Push-downs: how the cascade learned

The rule that makes a cascade get cheaper over time: every escalation ends with a change in a lower tier, so the same situation is routine next time. Over five days the Melvor agent logged 51 resolved escalations, each with what happened and what changed. This page shows who caught each kind of problem, where the fix landed, and the case studies behind most of the code.

Watch-band readings also show up inside Claude's audits: doubt rising to 0.64 in a buying loop, doubt flat at 0.60–0.63 on one pick, doubt 0.61 on a weak training recipe. In all, sub-threshold Jev readings were part of the evidence in at least 3 of the 37 audit catches.

The decision model judges the options it is given. Most problems were in the options, so most fixes were in code.

When Jev caught it

Jev doubt 0.79→LLM→Code

The 2% bow

The planner's first source for an Oak Shortbow was a monster that drops it once in about 50 kills. Doubt vetoed it. Fix: drops at 10% or more per kill count as a source; rarer drops come only after buying and level training.

Jev doubt 0.78→LLM→Code

Selling what you're making

The surplus-gear policy wanted to sell Leather Chaps every tick while Crafting was making them for XP. Fix: surplus sales need a nearly full bank and skip whatever the current action produces.

Jev doubt 0.84→LLM→Code

A ring with a rare input

Crafting training picked the Gold Ruby Ring as the best XP per material, but its only gold source was a rare drop. Fix: rare-drop steps are tagged, and training skips recipes that need one. The next pick scored doubt 0.41 with no escalation.

Jev novelty 0.71→LLM→Playbook

First time an ammo tier retired

After moving to Steel Arrows, Jev chose to sell 203 Bronze Arrows and old boots, and flagged it as new. Approved, and the playbook gained "older tiers are surplus once the better tier is worn". Retiring old armor later: novelty 0.35, no escalation.

Jev doubt 0.74→LLM→Playbook

A large, correct sale

Selling 247 Leather Chaps made for XP at a nearly full bank. It was fine, and approved for 18,525 gold. One playbook line ("XP-crafted gear stacks are surplus") brought the next similar sale to 0.35.

When the watch band pointed at it

doubt 0.60–0.63 ×3→LLM→Playbook

A playbook that contradicted itself

Jev kept picking Cows for Ranged training and doubting it each time, below the line. A playbook line said to prefer magic monsters, but none were safe. Adding the safety condition took doubt to 0.51, then 0.46.

doubt rising to 0.64→LLM→Code

A buying loop

An ammo bootstrap kept buying 300 Feathers whenever arrows were low, but the real block was Arrowtips; Headless Arrows piled up to 525. Fix: the bootstrap also requires few Headless Arrows.

novelty 0.62 · doubt 0.58→LLM→Code

A full restock for a small remainder

With 2,575 XP left to a milestone, the planner set out to make a standard 1,000 arrows. Both readings rose across the refill. Fix: when the milestone needs fewer arrows than the standard restock, make what it needs (at least 50).

Planner gaps Jev couldn't see

These reached Claude with doubt around 0.27–0.35. Jev picked the best of the options offered, but the option generator was wrong. Each was found by Claude reading the plan's reasons or the outcome, and each became a planner rule.

LLM audit→Code

Checked one craft, not the run

36 Feathers passed a "can make at least once" test, so the restock rule never ran. Fix: check inputs for the whole run and buy what's short first.

LLM audit→Code

Batch the chain

300 Headless Arrows but 90 Arrowtips would make 90 arrows, then a short fight. Fix: when a made input covers under half the run, make it first.

LLM audit→Code

One over the line

51 Feathers sat one above a "restock below 50" rule, which was enough for only 45 of the next 300 arrows. Fix: restock below one full batch.

LLM audit→Code Milestones

Batch sizes set the wake rate

A fixed batch of 120 meant about 12 rounds to Crafting 50; sizing it to the XP still needed (capped at 400) cut that to about 4. Raising the standing ammo goal from 300 to 1,000 cut about 16 refill cycles to about 5. Restocks now buy for the whole run, one purchase of 800 instead of three of 300.

LLM audit→Code

From hardcoded to measured targets

Ranged training was hardcoded to Cows, which wasted damage: max hit 118 against 80 HP. Fix: pick by damage per arrow. Then add the hit chance, because the model assumed every arrow hits. Finally, measured XP per arrow beats the model once both monsters have 50+ logged arrows. Measured 13.6 against 14.45 sent it back to the better monster.

LLM audit→Code

The wrong training metric

Smithing training offered Iron Daggers at 20 XP per bar while Mithril items paid 50, and one option counted XP per bar while ignoring the ore and coal behind each bar. Fix: rank by XP per unit still to be gathered, one recipe level down.

LLM audit→Code

Remembered recipe choices

Arrow Shafts can use any log; the panel kept the last choice (Willow) with 0 Willow Logs in the bank. Fix: read the game's per-recipe choice and pick the in-stock option. A related fix skips the category and recipe clicks when the recipe is already selected, which saved 2 of 4 steps on a restart.

LLM audit→Code

Verify that understands deliberate stops

Every Ranged round ended in a false failure, because the guard flees when the quiver is empty. Fix: guard and planner stops are recorded, and verify accepts them as success.

LLM audit→Code

Vanishing arrows

Crafts used their inputs and produced nothing: the bank was full, and the game discarded the output. Fix: a policy that buys bank slots before making anything when the bank is full. It later became "buy when two or fewer slots are free and gold is 2.5× the price".

When the environment broke

Environment→Code Human

Hidden tab, stalled game

The watcher reported stale snapshots four times. A per-second probe showed the tab going hidden for 32 seconds; the game paused, then ran an offline catch-up that ended fights. Fixes: status carries hidden, the watcher emits a hidden event, a resume guard restarts the fight, and the owner keeps the window visible.

Environment→Code

The wrong active tab

Opening a fresh browser window left a blank New Tab active, so clicks missed the game. Fix: a startup script closes blank tabs in the game's window and brings the game forward.

Environment→Code

The window shrank

At 844×476 the game folded its sidebar and switched to a mobile layout, so a sidebar button sat at x = −422. Fixes: locate returns the sidebar toggle when a target is off-screen, and takes the click tool's frame width so coordinates match after a resize.

Environment→Code

Saves that never happened

Force Save clicks missed for four hours unnoticed (stale coordinates, no check). Fixes: status reports minutes since the last cloud save, the watcher fires save-old at an hour, and every save is confirmed by the header resetting.

Environment→Code

The page moved under the cursor

Scroll anchoring shifted the page between locating a button and clicking it, so Loot All was missed by about 37 px. Fix: turn off scroll anchoring, and always locate a step right before clicking it.

When the human asked

Human→LLM→Code

"What should we be using money for?"

Answering it showed that no tool beyond Iron had ever been bought, though thousands of logs had been cut. No tier was comparing tools. Fix: a policy that buys the next axe, pickaxe or rod tier when it costs at most 25% of gold.

Human→LLM→Code

"Why am I not wearing a Mithril Helmet?"

Milestones named gear by hand, so nothing compared owned armor. Fix: a gear audit that scores pieces for the attack style, plus defence weighted by how dangerous the current enemy is. Under that score the Hard Leather Cowl correctly beats the Mithril Helmet against weak hitters.

Human approves→Code guard

A death-risk policy

Harder monsters pay more XP per arrow but can kill. The owner approved a plan, and it became code: flee if one enemy hit can take 40% of max HP, log arrows, food and deaths per monster, and discount XP by food eaten. Trials: one monster paid 19.6 XP an arrow but ate 1.9 food an arrow, so it was excluded; another paid 16.3 at 0.94.

Human→LLM→Code

"Is melee better for farming drops?"

Arrows were the bottleneck at 3.4 arrows per Leather. Character decision: farm drops with a blade. Fight steps now carry the weapon they want, and the brain swaps weapons first.

Human→LLM→Code

"Let's explore farming as passive income"

Farming chores have computable answers, so they went straight to code: harvest grown plots, clear dead ones, plant the highest-level seed, and unlock plots while they're cheap compared with gold. They click inside each plot card to avoid the paid "all plots" buttons.

What stays human

The runbook draws the human line in a few places, and nothing in five days moved it:

  • Real money and anything bought outside the game.
  • The account and its settings, and the owner's own save slot, which the agent never loads.
  • Anything irreversible outside the game. Inside the game, even expensive one-way choices stay with Claude. One example is a Township worship that costs 50M gold to change. The owner delegated the character.
  • Preferences: how the owner wants the character to play, and policies with real trade-offs, such as how much death risk is acceptable.
  • The physical environment: a covered or minimized window is the one failure no code can fix.

Lessons from the push-down log

  1. Audit plans, not just doubt. 37 of 51 catches came from the LLM reading plans and outcomes. Doubt can't see a bad option generator.
  2. Most fixes belong in code. 43 of 51 changed code. When a question has a computable answer, or Jev is wrong in a consistent direction, it moves down.
  3. Context fixes are cheap and measurable. One playbook line moved doubt from 0.74 to 0.35, or 0.63 to 0.46. Doubt works as a metric for context changes.
  4. Read the watch band. Flat mid-band doubt meant a context conflict, rising doubt meant a loop, and both stayed under 0.7.
  5. Sizes set the wake rate. Batches, restocks and standing goals sized to the real need cut LLM wakes by about a factor of three each time.
  6. The environment is its own failure class. Tabs, windows, layouts and saves each got a detector in code and a clear hand-off to a human.
