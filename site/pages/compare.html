Four ways compared

We ran the same harnesses against four ways of getting a typed judgment: Jev (TypeSafe, hosted), OpenAI's Decisions API, open-weight decision models you run yourself, and a small general LLM (Claude Haiku 5.5) asked to fill in the same answer shapes. Same questions, same labels, same grading.

A note on "Luna." gpt-6-luna is two things at OpenAI: the only model behind the Decisions API, and OpenAI's cheapest general-purpose text model on Chat Completions and Responses ($0.10 in / $0.50 out per million, released September 22, 2026). Called through /v1/decisions, it behaves like a decision model: deterministic, input-only pricing. Called as a chat model, it's in the same family as Haiku 5.5, a small general LLM. We measured the Decisions endpoint first; on October 27 we also asked it as a chat model, the way we ask Haiku, and it got markedly better on wine and mail at Haiku's costs ("Luna, Haiku-style" below; details).

What each one is

1. Jev: the reference

Strongest on real judgment where it matters most to us (mail concept detection and the sorter), fast, deterministic, pinnable, and the cheapest per token of any hosted option. Weak spots match its published jaggedness: temporal and numeric reasoning (0.27 on that JevBench family), long policies (0.63), multi-hop (0.83). Keep arithmetic and dates in code and it's hard to beat.

1b. Nimble v4-max: a better Jev at Jev's price

  • Best wine model on every arm (1.76 with 38 questions, 1.92 directly), and on top of all three mail concepts. It names the exact deadline class 67% of the time (Jev 43%) and holds up under rough phrasing: at or above Jev on 37 of 42 question variants, against Haiku's 36.
  • Jev's request shape, unchanged, at $0.04 per million input tokens and p50 0.37 s.
  • Risks: its mail sorter missed one more action than Jev's, re-asks are not always identical, the model name is the only pin, and the nimble-v4 and nimble-latest aliases point to the same model (there is no hosted 9B). See Nimble takes the lead.

2. OpenAI Decisions API: a near-twin, faster, a bit pricier

  • Ties Jev on wine: −0.02 RMSE with 38 questions (95% CI −0.07 to +0.02), +0.03 directly.
  • Agrees with Jev more than any challenger (median per-question correlation 0.91 on Score, 0.87 on Noul), yet adds information: Jev plus Luna answers together gave 1.81, against 1.86 for Jev alone.
  • Fastest hosted option measured: p50 0.30 s, 45 requests per second without a single 429.
  • Accepts images in the input; Jev is text-only.
  • Risks: no pinned model id (answers can move under a frozen threshold without notice), a different wire format (needs an adapter), it rejects one-option Choices, and the one forbidden click on the page chooser.

3. Open weights: free, private, and now level with Jev

  • APUS-OpenJev-4B on a Mac: about 5% worse than Jev with 38 questions combined, 23% worse as a single judge. Unbiased but noisier. 5–18 s per message. As a sorter feature source it filtered 46.8% at zero misses, respectable.
  • Strands Decider 2B: well calibrated (ECE 0.049) and fast on a GPU, but below Jev on every judgment task here and weakest as a feature source (oracle ceiling 15 points below APUS). It compresses degree: its Score answers cluster toward the middle. Fine for easy concepts (payment failures, waiting-on-reply) and for asking the score directly.
  • Early-October verdict: open weights were "mid": worth it for privacy or zero marginal cost, not for quality.
  • Two weeks later, that changed. Five fine-tuned 26–27B models (Kev-27B, d3, pplx-decider, JEV-27B-VL, GEV-26B) all screened at Jev's level on our private mail concepts and went 12/12 on the page chooser. Kev-27B, run in full, ties Jev on the sorter (ceiling 63.3% against 63.6%) and the 38-question wine arm, and beats it asking for the wine score directly (2.13 against 2.31).
  • The cost moved to hardware and speed. Each wants an 80 GB GPU at full precision. On one H100, GEV-26B and Kev answer a mail message in 0.3–0.4 s; d3 and pplx-decider take 4–5 s on their published NVIDIA path.

Update, October 14: we ran six of the new open models; results on Open weights caught up. The paragraph below is what we wrote on October 12. That may already be out of date. Since our runs, Strands shipped Gemma 4 sizes (the 26B-A4B reports 207/231 on JevBench, above Jev's 200), APUS added 9B and 35B-A3B sizes, and several 31B open models now score above Jev on JevBench's Capability board. We haven't run those on our harnesses yet, and the bigger ones need serious hardware: a 31B dense model wants roughly 17 GB at 4-bit and an 80 GB GPU at full precision. See Landscape and what it takes to run them.

4. Claude Haiku 5.5: the quality leader, at a price in money, time and repeatability

  • Ties or beats Jev on every harness. Far ahead on JevBench (0.97) and on phrasings Jev handles badly: at or above Jev on 36 of 42 mail ladder questions. The first challenger to go 12/12 on the page chooser, and it answers the one-option Choice the decision endpoints reject.
  • Best single ranker: "obligation" alone reached AUC 0.941 for ranking mail that needs action (Jev 0.799).
  • Costs: 5–7× Jev per request (still under $0.001), 3–5× the latency, and answers that wobble: a third of re-asks identical, yes-probabilities moving 0.10 at p90, 13% of Choice argmaxes flipping.
  • Its probabilities are numbers it writes down, not measured ones. It spreads them less than Jev (median Noul spread 0.16 against 0.22).
  • Luna and Terra, asked the same way (October 27): GPT-6 Luna ties Nimble on wine and Jev on the mail sorter (59.0% with one miss) at Haiku's price, but re-asks match only 16% of the time. GPT-6.1 Terra is steadier and tops JevBench (230/231) but costs about 40× Jev. See Nimble takes the lead.

When to use a small LLM (Haiku, or Luna as a chat model) instead of a decision model

Reach for Haiku 5.5 or a Luna-class LLM when

  • The judgment needs reasoning steps: multi-hop, long policies, dates, numbers, "probability" questions. Haiku scored 1.00 on JevBench families where Jev scored 0.27–0.76.
  • Your phrasing is still rough. Haiku is far more robust to imperfect questions; a decision model rewards careful, contrastive phrasing.
  • Volume is low (thousands a day, not millions) and a 2–3 s answer is fine. At $0.0004 a request, the money rarely matters.
  • You want one vendor and one model family for the LLM tier and the judgment tier, or you need images or long context.
  • You need a single strong ranker, not a feature set.
  • The answer feeds a human or an LLM that tolerates a little noise, not a frozen threshold.

Stay with a decision model when

  • A frozen threshold gates an action. You need the same answer every time.
  • Volume or latency dominates: millions of decisions, an agent tick, a page step. Decision endpoints are 0.3–0.6 s and a fraction of the price.
  • You're combining many questions as features. At 38 questions per item, 6× the price adds up, and the decision models already nearly match.
  • You need calibrated probabilities rather than written-down numbers.
  • The data must stay local: run an open-weight decision model.

A pragmatic hybrid: develop your questions against Haiku (it forgives rough phrasing and tells you whether the concept is learnable at all), then port the best phrasings to a decision model for production and re-grade them, because phrasing doesn't transfer between models unchanged. Or put Haiku one rung up: decision model first, Haiku for the watch band, a larger Claude for real escalations.
