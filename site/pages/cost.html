Read the estimates as floors. The frontier rows assume a frontier model answers the same small question with the same small output. In practice the LLM tier does bigger work (a deep read, a plan audit, a fix), with longer prompts and more thinking at higher effort, so one LLM escalation often costs 10× the per-decision figure above. That's another reason the cascade matters: the LLM's time is for problems only it can solve.

Two other prices to know. Microsoft's hosted Decision-1 (launched October 9) is listed at $0.042 per million input tokens, the same as Jev; we haven't tested it. Cloudflare's open Clef models are hosted on Workers AI at $0.24 (Clef) and $0.09 (Clef-flash) per million input tokens. Batch APIs halve the Claude prices (Haiku 5.5 on Batch: $0.05 / $0.25) when you can wait.

For scale: the operations agent's decision model cost under $0.10 per overnight session. A full day of wine-cookbook runs, including tests, came to $0.54 on Jev. The whole Haiku benchmark (15,039 calls) cost $7.57; the Decisions API benchmark (9,314 calls) cost $1.78. Screening six open 26–27B models on rented H100s took about $24 of GPU time. On October 27, Nimble's full wine, mail, page and JevBench runs cost $1.03 together; the same runs through Luna and Terra asked like Haiku cost $5.59 and $24.

Rented hardware is priced by the hour, not the request. The open rows assume the GPU is kept busy. At $3.29 an hour, an idle hour buys nothing, while the same money buys about 52,000 typical Jev requests. A rented open model beats hosted Jev on cost only if you own the GPU already or keep it near full; its stronger reasons are privacy and control.

Two things fall out quickly. Once people are in the loop, their time dominates every other cost, so the human tier should see only true preference and stakes. And the decision model's share barely moves the bill. What moves it is how many decisions escalate past it. That's why the push-down loop, which turns each escalation into a rule or a context line, is the real cost lever.

Latency climbs a similar ladder. Hook Chain's latency ladder normalizes to one pass-through Claude Code hook (about 42 µs): a call to Jev is about 14,000× that, and Haiku about 60,000×.

Choosing by cost alone is a mistake. Haiku costs 6× Jev but wins on reasoning-heavy judgments; the Decisions API costs 2.4× but is the fastest hosted option; a rented open 27B costs about 5× Jev but keeps the data in your hands; Nimble costs what Jev costs and beat it on most of our tests, but isn't perfectly repeatable.
